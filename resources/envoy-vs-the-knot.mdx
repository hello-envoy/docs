**Envoy vs The Knot** is a choice between two different jobs in wedding planning. The Knot is a large wedding marketplace: a vendor directory, couple reviews, and planning ideas, with websites and registries in the same company family. Envoy is a project workspace for the people running the wedding — you find **Contacts**, keep the details in one place, and send vendor outreach from your own email.

This page explains the major parts of both products and ends with a [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix). Use it to decide where to browse vendors at volume, and where to coordinate the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and everyone else.

## What The Knot is

The Knot is one of the best-known wedding planning destinations in the U.S. Couples use it to **find wedding vendors**, compare them, and collect ideas. It is part of The Knot Worldwide, which also operates WeddingWire and other wedding brands.

### Wedding vendor directory

The directory is the heart of the product. You can browse venues, photographers, florists, caterers, and other wedding professionals by category and location. Listings typically include photos, couple reviews, and details that help you narrow a long list. The brand reach is the point: you can see a high volume of vendors in one place before you know who belongs on your shortlist.

### Planning ideas and tools

Alongside the directory, The Knot publishes wedding ideas, real-wedding stories, and planning guidance. Couples also get planning tools such as checklists that sit next to that content. This is general wedding planning help — useful while you are still learning what the day should include.

### Websites, registries, and invitations

The Knot Worldwide also offers guest-facing pieces many couples expect from a wedding suite: a wedding website, a registry, and invitations. Those tools serve guests and gifts. They are part of why people stay inside that ecosystem after the first vendor search.

### How couples and listed businesses use it

A typical couple path is: browse a category, read reviews, and request information from listings you like. Messages and quote requests happen in the marketplace.

On the business side, vendors pay to advertise and to receive leads. A premium listing is how many professionals get in front of engaged couples. That model is good for discovery. The introduction is still a marketplace lead, which is a different path from emailing someone from your own inbox.

**The Knot is for** couples who want a famous directory, reviews, and wedding inspiration, and who are comfortable requesting information through that marketplace.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for **running the wedding as a project** and contacting the service providers on it. In the product, those service providers are **Contacts** — the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and anyone else you need.

Wedding websites, registries, and RSVPs stay with a product built for guests. Envoy is the coordination and vendor-outreach layer.

### Project workspace

Each wedding is a project with three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | Title, description, location, budget, deadline, and the contacts on this wedding |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy — scope, priorities, and what to ask before you write |
| **Outreach** | Review drafts, revise them, send, and follow up when someone replies |

The same date, location, budget, and deadline stay attached to every contact on that project. You are not retyping the wedding into a separate form for each vendor category.

### Contacts and discovery

On the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), describe the wedding and enter a ZIP or postal code before you create an account. Envoy returns service providers grouped by category. Badges such as **Onboarded to Envoy** and **Unverified listing** show how each result is listed. You can select up to 8 contacts before you sign up. After you sign in, search again from **Dashboard** while you create the project, or add people later from **Contacts**.

You choose who goes on the project, and the email comes from you.

### Chat

**Chat** is scoped to this project. It uses the title, description, location, budget, and deadline you saved. Ask what to send a venue first, what a caterer needs to quote, or what to prioritize if the date is close. When planning is complete, Envoy prepares outreach drafts for the contacts on the project.

### Outreach from your inbox

Connect Gmail or Outlook under **Connected Email Accounts**. Envoy drafts the email from the project. You read it, use **Revise with Envoy** to change tone or fill in missing details, and click **Approve & Send**. The message comes from your address. Replies return to your inbox and appear on the **Outreach** tab, where you can continue the thread.

**Envoy is for** the couple, or someone coordinating for the couple, who wants one workspace for a multi-vendor wedding and email they control.

## How you find wedding vendors

The Knot is built for **breadth**. Open a category, scan a large directory, and use reviews and photos to learn the market in your city. That is the fastest way to see vendor options at volume under a brand couples already know.

Envoy is built for a **shortlist tied to a project**. You describe the wedding, search by location, and keep the people you might hire as Contacts. Homepage search works before you sign up, so you can tell whether the results fit before you create an account.

Use The Knot when you still need the lay of the land. Use Envoy when you are ready to name the people on the wedding and keep them together.

## How you coordinate a multi-vendor wedding

A wedding is several jobs that share one date. The venue, florist, photographer, and caterer all need the same location, headcount context, budget, and deadline.

On The Knot, that work is organized as directory categories and the inquiries you send from each listing. Planning content and checklists help you remember the tasks. The conversations stay attached to the marketplace listing you contacted.

On Envoy, those people are contacts on **one project**. **Overview** holds the details. **Chat** helps you decide what each contact needs to hear. You update the project once, and the next draft can use those details. This is the stronger fit when coordination — not browsing — is the work in front of you.

## How vendor outreach and follow-up work

The Knot outreach is a **marketplace request**. You ask a listing for information, and the thread lives on The Knot. Listed businesses often pay for that introduction. For a first look, that is simple: you do not have to write a full email to everyone in a category.

Envoy outreach is **your email**. You pick the contact, approve the draft, and send it from Gmail or Outlook. Couples pay no lead fee on either product. On Envoy the introduction is your message, sent to a contact you chose. Follow-up happens in the same thread, in your inbox and on **Outreach**. That is the stronger fit when you want a written record you own and a message you signed off on.

## What couples and pros pay for

Couples can browse The Knot's directory and request information without paying a lead fee. The business model sits with vendors: advertising and leads are how many listings get distribution.

On Envoy, contact search on the homepage is free before you sign up. You sign in with Google or Outlook when you are ready to create the project and send outreach. Envoy's consumer product has no vendor lead-fee step between you and the contact.

## Conclusion

Each product covers a different part of wedding planning. **The Knot** is the stronger place to browse wedding vendors at scale, read reviews, and gather ideas. Websites, registries, and invitations from that ecosystem are there when you want them. **Envoy** is the stronger place to run the wedding as a project: one set of details, contacts you choose, and vendor outreach from your own inbox.

Use The Knot for the directory. Use Envoy for the shortlist and the emails. Many couples keep both.

### Comparison matrix

Envoy is the stronger fit for coordinating the wedding and contacting people. The Knot is the stronger fit for directory scale. That is the one category below where The Knot has the edge.

| Category | Envoy | The Knot | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-contact project workspace | One project for the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and other contacts, with shared location, budget, and deadline | Category browsing and a separate inquiry for each listing | **Envoy** |
| Wedding directory scale | Contact search for the wedding you describe | One of the largest U.S. wedding vendor directories, with the brand reach to browse vendors at volume and read couple reviews | **The Knot** |
| You choose who to contact | You pick the contacts and decide who receives an email | You choose listings to inquire about; many businesses pay to advertise and receive those leads | **Envoy** |
| Outreach from your own email | Drafts you approve and send from Gmail or Outlook, with **Revise with Envoy** | Requests and messages inside the marketplace | **Envoy** |
| Project-scoped planning | **Chat** on this wedding, using your saved details, before drafts go out | Articles, checklists, and ideas for couples in general | **Envoy** |
| Contact shortlist before signup | Homepage search and a shortlist before you create an account; later, search from **Dashboard** | A public directory you can browse while you are still deciding | **Envoy** |
| Follow-up in your inbox | Replies in your email and on the project's **Outreach** tab | Follow-up in The Knot messages | **Envoy** |
