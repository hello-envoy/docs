Keep **The Knot** for browsing wedding vendors, couple reviews, and ideas. Use **Envoy** when you're ready to email the people you've shortlisted. You don't have to switch.

Using Envoy does not replace The Knot — most couples keep both.

A couple can do this, or a planner can coordinate the wedding for you and send from their inbox.

Wedding vendors (called **Contacts** in Envoy) are the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and the other businesses on your wedding. After this, the pages say Contacts for those vendors. They are not your personal address book.

The Knot is the biggest-known brand when you want to browse everything. [WeddingWire](/resources/envoy-vs-weddingwire) is the sister site for local listings you compare side by side and request quotes from. Same company. Different job.

## The Knot vs Envoy

The Knot is where you browse wedding vendors, read couple reviews, and collect ideas. Envoy is where you keep a shortlist on one wedding project and email from Gmail or Outlook. Most couples keep both.

## At a glance

- **Keep The Knot for** the wide directory, couple reviews, and wedding inspiration.
- **Use Envoy for** one wedding project and email from your inbox, once you have a shortlist.
- **Don't expect Envoy to** replace The Knot's reviews, ideas, wedding website, or registry. Deposits, contracts, and the wedding-day plan stay with your vendors and their own tools.

Details are below. The [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) is at the end.

## What The Knot is

The Knot is one of the best-known wedding planning destinations in the U.S. Couples use it to **find wedding vendors**, compare them, and collect ideas. It is part of The Knot Worldwide, which also operates WeddingWire and other wedding brands.

### Wedding vendor directory

The directory is the heart of the product. You can browse venues, photographers, florists, caterers, and other wedding professionals by category and location. Listings typically include photos, couple reviews, and details that help you narrow a long list. The brand reach is the point: you can see a high volume of vendors in one place before you know who belongs on your shortlist.

### Planning ideas and tools

Alongside the directory, The Knot publishes wedding ideas, real-wedding stories, and planning guidance. Couples also get planning tools such as checklists that sit next to that content. This is general wedding planning help — useful while you are still learning what the day should include.

### Websites, registries, and invitations

The Knot Worldwide also offers guest-facing pieces many couples expect from a wedding suite: a wedding website, a registry, and invitations. Those tools serve guests and gifts. They are part of why people stay inside that ecosystem after the first vendor search.

### How couples and listed businesses use it

A typical couple path is: browse a category, read reviews, and request information from listings you like. Messages and quote requests happen in the marketplace.

You do choose who to contact on The Knot. You pick listings and request information. Browsing is free for couples. Vendors often pay for those leads. On Envoy the email is yours — that is the difference, not "who gets to choose."

**The Knot is for** couples and planners who want a famous directory, couple reviews, and wedding inspiration, and who are fine requesting information in the marketplace.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for **running the wedding as a project** and contacting the service providers on it. In the product, those service providers are **Contacts** — the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and anyone else you need.

Wedding websites, registries, and RSVPs stay on The Knot (or Zola or Joy). Envoy is the coordination and vendor-outreach layer.

A planner can run the project for the couple: add the vendors, keep the date and budget on the wedding, and send from the inbox they connect.

### Project workspace

Each wedding is a project with three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | Title, description, location, budget, deadline, and the contacts on this wedding |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy — scope, priorities, and what to ask before you write |
| **Outreach** | Review drafts, revise them, send, and follow up when someone replies |

The same date, location, budget, and deadline stay attached to every contact on that project. You are not retyping the wedding into a separate form for each vendor category.

### Contacts and discovery

On the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), describe the wedding and enter a ZIP or postal code before you create an account. Results are grouped by category. You can start a shortlist (up to 8 before you create an account). That limit applies only to the search you do before sign-in, so you can try Envoy without an account. After you sign in, add more from **Dashboard**, the **Contacts** page, or the project. It is not a forever ceiling.

Search follows the description and location you type. A thin result in your city means try a more specific description, or add the vendor you already know. Envoy is not The Knot's catalog, and it does not include Knot reviews.

**Onboarded to Envoy** means the business confirmed this listing. **Unverified listing** means someone else added it and the business has not confirmed it. It is OK to email an unverified listing — check the address yourself first. Neither badge tells you when they will reply. See [Contact badges explained](/help-center/contacts/contact-badges-explained).

Vendors you already have do not have to come from search. On **Contacts**, click **+ New contact** and enter a name and email. On the project **Overview** tab, use **+ New contact** or **+ Attach existing** to put someone from your directory on the wedding. A Knot favorite you want to email is added by you; Envoy does not import The Knot saves.

### Chat

**Chat** is scoped to this project. It uses the title, description, location, budget, and deadline you saved. Ask what to send a venue first, what a caterer needs to quote, or what to prioritize if the date is close. When planning is complete, Envoy prepares outreach drafts for the contacts on the project.

### A pass through one wedding

**Overview:** a Charleston weekend wedding, about 120 guests. Venue and photographer first, then florist, caterer, and others, with budget and the date you need answers. **Chat:** "What should the first note to the photographer include?" **Outreach:** Envoy drafts that note, you use **Revise with Envoy** if it is too long, then **Approve & Send**. The reply shows in your inbox and on **Outreach**. The Knot stays open for reviews.

### Outreach from your inbox

Envoy only uses email related to the contacts you selected. It can prepare drafts, send messages you approve, and show those threads in **Outreach**. Disconnect the inbox anytime from **Account**.

Connect Gmail or Outlook under **Connected Email Accounts**. The message comes from your address. You approve it before it sends.

**Envoy is for** the couple, or a planner sending for them, who wants one workspace for the wedding's vendors and email they control.

## How you find wedding vendors

The Knot is built for **breadth and couple reviews**. Open a category, scan a large directory, and read what other couples wrote. That review library is a real reason to stay on The Knot (and on WeddingWire). Envoy badges tell you whether a listing is claimed. They are not a substitute for those reviews.

Envoy is built for a **shortlist tied to a project**, including vendors you type in yourself. Homepage search works before you sign up, so you can see whether results in your area are useful before you create an account.

Use The Knot for the lay of the land and the reviews. Use Envoy to name the people on the wedding and email them.

## How you coordinate a multi-vendor wedding

A wedding is several jobs that share one date. The venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and the other vendors all need the same location, headcount context, budget, and deadline.

On The Knot, that work is organized as directory categories and the inquiries you send from each listing. Planning content and checklists help you remember the tasks. The conversations stay attached to the marketplace listing you contacted.

On Envoy, those people are contacts on **one project**. **Overview** holds the details. **Chat** helps you decide what each contact needs to hear. You update the project once, and the next draft can use those details. Browsing can stay on The Knot while this coordination happens in Envoy.

## How vendor outreach and follow-up work

The Knot outreach is a **marketplace request**. You choose a listing and ask for information. The thread lives on The Knot. For a first look at a category, that is simple.

Envoy outreach is **your email** to a contact you picked — including one you added by name. You approve the draft and send it from Gmail or Outlook. Replies come back to that inbox and to the **Outreach** tab.

## What couples and pros pay for

Searching for wedding vendors is free for couples before and after you sign up. You can keep searching from **Dashboard**, while you create a project, and from **Contacts**. These Help pages aren't a full pricing sheet — plan or account options show in the product when you sign in with Google or Outlook.

<Note>
  **A typical planning week:** guest list and registry on Zola or Joy, browse vendors at volume on The Knot or WeddingWire, shortlist and email on Envoy, contracts and invoices in the pro's tools (often HoneyBook).
</Note>

## Conclusion

Keep The Knot for reviews, ideas, and browsing at volume. Use Envoy when you're ready to email people you've shortlisted. You don't have to switch. Websites and registries in The Knot's ecosystem can stay there too.

### Comparison matrix

This isn't a winner-take-all score — each product wins the job it's built for. The Knot's rows come first: reviews, inspiration, and browsing a huge directory. Envoy's rows are the project and the email. You still choose listings on The Knot. The email row is a marketplace request versus a note from your inbox.

| Category | Envoy | The Knot | Best for… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Couple reviews and wedding ideas | Badges show whether a listing is confirmed. They are not reviews or real-wedding stories | A large library of couple reviews, photos, and inspiration | **The Knot** |
| Browse wedding vendors at volume | Search for the wedding you describe. Results depend on your words and city, and they are not The Knot's catalog | The brand couples already know when they want to browse everything in a category | **The Knot** |
| Email a shortlist from your inbox | You approve a Gmail or Outlook note to a vendor you searched or typed in. Browsing is free for couples on both. On The Knot, vendors often pay for leads. On Envoy the email is yours | You choose a listing and request information inside The Knot | **Envoy** |
| One wedding, many vendors | Venue, photographer, florist, caterer, and others share the date, city, budget, and deadline | A separate inquiry for each category | **Envoy** |
| Start a shortlist before an account | Up to 8 vendors before you create an account. Add more after you sign in | Browse the public directory while you decide | **Envoy** |
| Plan what this wedding should ask | **Chat** on your saved details, then **Revise with Envoy** | Articles and checklists for couples in general | **Envoy** |
| Vendors you already know | **+ New contact** or **+ Attach existing** with a name and email | Favorites stay on The Knot. Add the ones you want on the Envoy project yourself | **Envoy** |
| Where a reply shows up | Your inbox and the project's **Outreach** thread | Messages on The Knot | **Envoy** |

<Tip>
  Keep The Knot open for reviews and ideas. When you want to email a shortlist, try the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com) — search is free before you sign up, and the 8-vendor limit is only that first shortlist. You don't have to switch.
</Tip>
