Keep **Joy** for your wedding website, guest list, and RSVPs. Use **Envoy** when you're ready to email wedding vendors you've shortlisted. You don't have to switch.

Using Envoy does not replace Joy — most couples keep both.

A couple can do this, or a planner can coordinate the wedding for you and send from their inbox.

Wedding vendors (called **Contacts** in Envoy) are the people doing the work: venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and other service providers. In Envoy that is the project list. It is not your guest address book and not Joy's RSVP list.

## Joy vs Envoy

Joy holds the wedding website, guest list, and RSVPs. Envoy is where you email wedding vendors, including ones you already booked. Most couples keep both.

## At a glance

- **Keep Joy for** the site guests open, the guest list, and RSVPs.
- **Use Envoy for** emailing vendors, including ones you already booked.
- **Don't expect Envoy to** publish your wedding website or collect RSVPs. Deposits, contracts, and the wedding-day plan stay with your vendors and their own tools.

The [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) is at the end. Guest tools stay on Joy.

## What Joy is

Joy (withjoy.com) is built around **the experience guests have**. Couples use it to publish a wedding website and to collect responses, with the guest list as the system of record for who was invited.

### Wedding website

The website is the front door. You publish the story, the schedule, travel, and event details, and you send guests one link. Joy's reputation is a clean guest-facing site that is straightforward to share. That public page is the product people mean when they say they are "using Joy."

### Guest list

The guest list is where you track households, groups, and events. A wedding with a welcome party and a ceremony can keep those lists related without turning into a pile of spreadsheets. Address collection and who-is-invited-to-what live here, next to the site.

### RSVPs

RSVP tracking shows who has responded and who has not. The list and the website stay in sync with those replies, which is the tedious part of guest management if you try to do it from an inbox alone. Joy is strongest when the next task is "hear back from guests," not "email five vendors."

### Service providers, lightly

Joy can help you keep track of professionals you have already chosen, beside the guest plan. A large vendor directory and an inbox for vendor outreach are a separate job. Couples who still need to **find wedding vendors** and write to them do that work in a tool built for it.

**Joy is for** couples who want a wedding website, a guest list, and RSVPs in one guest-first product.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for **contacting and coordinating service providers** while you plan the wedding. In the product they are **Contacts**: venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and anyone else on the project.

Joy keeps the guest website and the RSVPs. Envoy keeps the private plan and the vendor outreach.

A planner can run the Envoy project for the couple and send from their own inbox. The couple can keep the Joy site.

### Project workspace

Every project has three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | The wedding details contacts need, and the people attached to the project |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy before the first email |
| **Outreach** | Review Envoy-drafted messages, send them, and reply |

Guests see a website. Contacts see an email that came from you, backed by the details on **Overview**.

### Contacts and discovery

On the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), describe the wedding and search by location before you create an account. You can start a shortlist (up to 8 before you create an account). That limit is only for the try-it search before sign-in. After you sign in, add more from **Dashboard**, from **Contacts**, or on the project. It is not a forever ceiling.

**Onboarded to Envoy** means the business confirmed this listing. **Unverified listing** means someone else added it and the business has not confirmed it. It is OK to email an unverified listing — check the address yourself first. Neither badge tells you when they will reply. See [Contact badges explained](/help-center/contacts/contact-badges-explained).

If the caterer is already booked, add them directly: **+ New contact** with name and email, or **+ Attach existing** on **Overview**. Search is optional. Joy can keep a note beside the guest list; Envoy is where that caterer gets the email. Results also depend on your description and city — a thin search is a reason to type the vendor in, not a claim that Envoy matches every market Joy's couples plan in.

### Chat

**Chat** is limited to this project. It can see your title, description, location, budget, and deadline. Use it to prepare different questions for a venue and a band, or to decide what the first email must include. When planning is finished, Envoy can draft outreach for the contacts on the project.

### A pass through one wedding

**Overview:** a Charleston weekend wedding, about 120 guests. Venue and photographer first. The photographer is someone you typed in. Budget stays off the public site. **Chat:** "What can we ask the photographer without putting it on the Joy website?" **Outreach:** draft, **Revise with Envoy**, **Approve & Send**. They reply to you. Guests still RSVP on Joy.

### Outreach from your inbox

Envoy only uses email related to the contacts you selected. It prepares drafts, sends the ones you approve, and shows those threads in **Outreach**. Disconnect anytime from **Account**. Guest mail on Joy is a different product.

Under **Connected Email Accounts**, connect Gmail or Outlook. **Approve & Send** goes from your address.

**Envoy is for** the couple, or a planner helping them, who needs vendor threads in one project. Joy is still the site.

## The guest experience

Joy's website, guest list, and RSVPs are the part of wedding planning guests touch. You send a link, they respond, and you can see the gaps before the deadline. Groups and events give you a way to invite the right people to the right gathering.

If the job this week is the guest experience, set up Joy. Envoy has no public wedding page and no RSVP form to hand anyone.

## Finding and contacting wedding vendors

Joy is comfortable when you **already know** the professionals and want a light note beside the site. The emails themselves go from Envoy, including to vendors you add by name.

You can also **find wedding vendors** by describing the project on the homepage before you sign up, then keep going after sign-in. Vendor outreach is the draft, a revision if you want one, and a send from your own inbox. Browsing is free for you here. This is not a lead marketplace: the email is yours.

That is why the two products sit side by side instead of competing for the same screen. Guests get Joy. The caterer gets an email from you.

## Where the planning details live

A guest site tells one public story: when, where, and what to wear. Contacts usually need a private version: budget, deadline, headcount, and what you want from them. Those facts do not belong on the website.

Envoy stores that private version on the project. **Chat** helps you decide what to reveal in the first email. **Outreach** sends it only to the contact you picked. You can keep the public site on Joy and still have one place that remembers what you told the florist versus the photographer.

Follow-up with guests (RSVP reminders, site updates) stays in Joy. Follow-up with vendors stays in your inbox.

## What couples pay attention to

Joy is something you set up so guests have a home base. The value is the site and the responses.

Envoy is what you open when you are coordinating the wedding team. Searching for wedding vendors is free for couples before and after you sign up, from **Dashboard**, project creation, and **Contacts** once you have an account. When you sign in with Google or Outlook, plan or account options show in the product. You approve every outreach message.

<Note>
  **A typical planning week:** the website, guest list, and RSVPs on Joy (registry on Zola if gifts live there), browsing on The Knot or WeddingWire, vendor email on Envoy, the vendor's contract in their own tools such as HoneyBook.
</Note>

## Conclusion

Keep the website, guest list, and RSVPs on Joy. Use Envoy when you're ready to email vendors. You don't have to switch.

### Comparison matrix

This isn't a winner-take-all score — each product wins the job it's built for. Joy's rows are the guest experience, and they come first. Envoy's rows are private vendor email. An Envoy row is not a guest-tool win.

| Category | Envoy | Joy | Best for… |
|---|---|---|---|
| The website guests open | Vendor email only. The public page stays on Joy | The link you send for the schedule, travel, and story | **Joy** |
| Guest list and RSVPs | Not where guests reply. RSVPs stay on Joy | Who is invited, who responded, and which event they're coming to | **Joy** |
| A vendor you already booked | **+ New contact** with their email, then a draft from the project | A light note beside the guest plan | **Envoy** |
| Email from your inbox | **Revise with Envoy**, then send from Gmail or Outlook after you approve | Guest-facing pages and RSVP messages | **Envoy** |
| Details that stay off the website | **Chat** helps you decide what the vendor email should say | The schedule and wording guests see | **Envoy** |
| Start a vendor shortlist first | Up to 8 vendors before you create an Envoy account. Add more after you sign in | The Joy site starts when you create the wedding there | **Envoy** |
| Two kinds of replies | Vendor replies in your inbox and on **Outreach** | Guest RSVPs on Joy | **Envoy** |

<Tip>
  Keep Joy for the guest site. Use Envoy when you're ready to email vendors — search on the [homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), or type in someone you already hired. You don't have to switch.
</Tip>

## Before you switch tools

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is search free after I sign up?">
    Searching for vendors is free for couples before and after you sign up. Search stays available from **Dashboard**, project creation, and **Contacts**. When you sign in with Google or Outlook, plan or account options show in the product.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Envoy replace The Knot or WeddingWire for finding vendors?">
    No. Those directories are larger, and they have couple reviews. Envoy search follows your description and city. If a vendor is missing, add them by name and email. Keep Joy for guests, and keep The Knot or WeddingWire for browsing and reviews.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can my partner and I share a project?">
    One person runs the Envoy project: you, or a planner, friend, or family member coordinating for you. They send from the inbox they connect. There is no shared login and no invite that adds your partner to that project. Your partner can keep the Joy site. Envoy is for finding and emailing vendors from the coordinator's inbox.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if a vendor never replies?">
    Envoy can't make them reply. Follow up on **Outreach**, or email someone else. It is OK to email an **Unverified listing** after you check the address. **Onboarded to Envoy** does not mean they will write back.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I lose my Joy site if I start Envoy?">
    No. Using Envoy does not replace Joy. The website, guest list, and RSVPs stay on Joy. Most couples keep both.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can our planner run Envoy and leave Joy with us?">
    Yes. The planner can send vendor email from their connected inbox. You keep the Joy website and RSVPs.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can we keep Joy and The Knot and still use Envoy?">
    Yes. Joy for guests, The Knot for browsing and reviews, Envoy for the vendors you email. Add people with search or **+ New contact**. Envoy does not import those lists.
  </Accordion>
