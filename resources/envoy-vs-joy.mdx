**Envoy vs Joy** is a guest-site question and a vendor-outreach question. Joy is a wedding website and guest-planning product: the page guests open, the guest list, and RSVPs. Envoy is where you find **Contacts** for the wedding and send vendor outreach from your own email.

Joy is lighter on discovering and emailing service providers. This page walks through both products and ends with a [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) so you can keep the guest experience and the vendor emails in the right place.

## What Joy is

Joy (withjoy.com) is built around **the experience guests have**. Couples use it to publish a wedding website and to collect responses, with the guest list as the system of record for who was invited.

### Wedding website

The website is the front door. You publish the story, the schedule, travel, and event details, and you send guests one link. Joy's reputation is a clean guest-facing site that is straightforward to share. That public page is the product people mean when they say they are "using Joy."

### Guest list

The guest list is where you track households, groups, and events. A wedding with a welcome party and a ceremony can keep those lists related without turning into a pile of spreadsheets. Address collection and who-is-invited-to-what live here, next to the site.

### RSVPs

RSVP tracking shows who has responded and who has not. The list and the website stay in sync with those replies, which is the tedious part of guest management if you try to do it from an inbox alone. Joy is strongest when the next task is "hear back from guests," not "email five vendors."

### Service providers, lightly

Joy can help you keep track of professionals you have already chosen, beside the guest plan. A large vendor directory and an inbox for vendor outreach are a separate job. Couples who still need to **find wedding vendors** and write to them do that work in a tool built for it.

**Joy is for** couples who want a wedding website, a guest list, and RSVPs in one guest-first product.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for **contacting and coordinating service providers** while you plan the wedding. In the product they are **Contacts**: venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and anyone else on the project.

Joy keeps the guest website and the RSVPs. Envoy keeps the private plan and the vendor outreach.

### Project workspace

Every project has three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | The wedding details contacts need, and the people attached to the project |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy before the first email |
| **Outreach** | Review Envoy-drafted messages, send them, and reply |

Guests see a website. Contacts see an email that came from you, backed by the details on **Overview**.

### Contacts and discovery

On the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), describe the wedding and search by location before you create an account. You can select up to 8 contacts to carry into signup. Badges such as **Onboarded to Envoy** and **Unverified listing** tell you how a result is listed. From **Dashboard**, search again while you create the project. Add or attach more people later from **Contacts**.

You decide who is on the list.

### Chat

**Chat** is limited to this project. It can see your title, description, location, budget, and deadline. Use it to prepare different questions for a venue and a band, or to decide what the first email must include. When planning is finished, Envoy can draft outreach for the contacts on the project.

### Outreach from your inbox

Under **Connected Email Accounts**, connect Gmail or Outlook. Use **Revise with Envoy** if you want a different tone or more detail, then **Approve & Send**. The thread is with that contact. They reply to you, and the reply shows in your inbox and on **Outreach**.

**Envoy is for** the couple, or someone helping them, who still needs to reach service providers and keep those conversations together.

## The guest experience

Joy's website, guest list, and RSVPs are the part of wedding planning guests touch. You send a link, they respond, and you can see the gaps before the deadline. Groups and events give you a way to invite the right people to the right gathering.

Envoy never replaces that link. There is no public page and no RSVP form. If your immediate job is the guest experience, Joy is the product to set up.

## Finding and contacting wedding vendors

Joy assumes you often **already know** the professionals, and it gives you a light place to track them beside the site. It will not search a city for caterers and then send the emails.

Envoy starts from the other end. You **find wedding vendors** by describing the project and searching — including on the homepage before signup — and you turn the results into Contacts. Vendor outreach is the main action: a draft based on the project, a revision if you want one, and a send from your own inbox.

That is why the two products sit side by side instead of competing for the same screen. Guests get Joy. The caterer gets an email from you.

## Where the planning details live

A guest site tells one public story: when, where, and what to wear. Contacts usually need a private version: budget, deadline, headcount, and what you want from them. Those facts do not belong on the website.

Envoy stores that private version on the project. **Chat** helps you decide what to reveal in the first email. **Outreach** sends it only to the contact you picked. You can keep the public site on Joy and still have one place that remembers what you told the florist versus the photographer.

Follow-up with guests (RSVP reminders, site updates) stays in Joy. Follow-up with vendors stays in your inbox.

## What couples pay attention to

Joy is something you set up so guests have a home base. The value is the site and the responses.

Envoy is something you open when you are coordinating the wedding team. Homepage contact search is free before you sign up. You sign in with Google or Outlook to create the project and send mail. You approve every outreach message. There is no marketplace lead between you and the contact.

## Conclusion

**Joy** is the stronger product for the wedding website, the guest list, and RSVPs. **Envoy** is the stronger product for wedding planning that means finding contacts and running vendor outreach from your own email.

Publish the site in Joy. Build the shortlist and send the emails in Envoy. Guests and vendors need different tools, and these two cover those jobs without overlapping them.

### Comparison matrix

Envoy has the edge wherever the work is a project and an email to a service provider. Joy has the edge on exactly one category: the guest experience.

| Category | Envoy | Joy | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-contact project workspace | One wedding project for every service provider you need to email, with shared details | A guest plan organized around the website, events, and who is invited | **Envoy** |
| Wedding website, guest list, and RSVPs | Coordination and email for contacts; the public site lives elsewhere | The site guests open, the guest list, and RSVP tracking in one product | **Joy** |
| You choose who to contact | You search, select contacts, and decide who gets an email | You keep notes on professionals you have already chosen | **Envoy** |
| Outreach from your own email | **Revise with Envoy**, then send from Gmail or Outlook after you approve | Guest-facing pages and RSVPs | **Envoy** |
| Project-scoped planning | **Chat** answers questions for this wedding before you write | Planning that wraps the guest list and the site | **Envoy** |
| Contact shortlist before signup | Homepage search and a shortlist of up to 8 contacts before an account | The website starts when you create a Joy wedding | **Envoy** |
| Follow-up in your inbox | Vendor replies in your email and on **Outreach** | Guest responses collected as RSVPs | **Envoy** |

<Tip>
  Let Joy hold the guest site. When you are ready to reach service providers, start with contact search on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com).
</Tip>
