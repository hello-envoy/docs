Keep **WeddingWire** for local wedding-vendor listings you can compare and request quotes from. Use **Envoy** when you're ready to email the people you've shortlisted. You don't have to switch.

Using Envoy does not replace WeddingWire — most couples keep both.

A couple can do this, or a planner can coordinate the wedding for you and send from their inbox.

Wedding vendors (called **Contacts** in Envoy) are the businesses on the wedding — venue, photographer, florist, caterer. After this, these pages say Contacts. They are not a personal address book.

The Knot is the biggest-known brand when you want to browse everything. WeddingWire is the local one: open listings side by side, read what nearby couples wrote, and request a quote. They share a parent company, The Knot Worldwide, so some names overlap. This page is not The Knot with another logo. If you want the famous directory at volume, read [Envoy vs The Knot](/resources/envoy-vs-the-knot). Stay here when the next click is a local profile.

## At a glance

- **Keep WeddingWire for** comparing local vendors, couple reviews on those listings, and quote requests.
- **Use Envoy for** putting your shortlist on one wedding and emailing from your inbox.
- **Don't expect Envoy to** be WeddingWire's directory, reviews, or quote inbox. Deposits, contracts, and the wedding-day plan stay with your vendors and their own tools.

The [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) is at the end.

## What WeddingWire is

WeddingWire is a directory built so couples can **find wedding vendors** near them and compare listings before they book. The about-the-brand promise is search, comparison, and a path to local professionals — venues through photographers and everyone in between.

### Wedding vendor directory

You search by the kind of professional and the place you are getting married. Listings are meant to be compared side by side: photos, couple reviews, and pricing or availability details when the business has added them. That listing page is the product. It answers "who works in my area, and what do other couples say?"

### Planning tools, ideas, and community

WeddingWire also offers planning tools, inspiration, and a reviews community around the directory. Couples use them to keep a checklist in view while they are still requesting information. The center of gravity remains the marketplace: listings, reviews, and quote requests.

### A sibling of The Knot, for local listings

WeddingWire and The Knot belong to the same company, The Knot Worldwide, so some business names show up on both. That overlap is not a second copy of The Knot. The Knot is where you browse the big brand. WeddingWire's job here is the local listing: compare profiles side by side, read what nearby couples wrote, and request a quote.

### How couples and listed businesses use it

Couples search the directory and request information from the profiles they like. The conversation starts as a WeddingWire inquiry.

You choose which listings to message. Browsing is free for couples. Vendors often pay for leads. On Envoy the email is yours. Choice is not the split — a marketplace request versus your own email is.

**WeddingWire is for** couples and planners who want local wedding listings, couple reviews, and quote requests in that marketplace.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for the person **planning the wedding as a project** and contacting service providers directly. In Envoy those businesses are **Contacts**. A wedding project can hold the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and the rest together.

Keep the guest website, registry, and RSVPs on Zola or Joy. Envoy is where vendor outreach lives.

A planner can send for the couple from the inbox they connect, with the wedding's details on the project.

### Project workspace

Open the project and you get three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | Project details — location, budget, deadline — and the contacts attached to this wedding |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy using the details you already saved |
| **Outreach** | Drafts, revisions, sends, and replies |

Shared details stay on the project. You do not rebuild the brief every time you move from the florist listing to the caterer listing.

### Contacts and discovery

Describe the wedding on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com) and search by ZIP or postal code with no account. You can start a shortlist (up to 8 before you create an account). That limit is only so you can try a search before sign-in. After you sign in, add more from **Dashboard**, **Contacts**, or the project. It is not a cap on the wedding.

Results depend on what you type and where the wedding is. Envoy is not WeddingWire's local catalog. If someone is missing, add the vendor you already saved: **+ New contact** (name and email) on **Contacts**, or **+ New contact** / **+ Attach existing** on **Overview**. Envoy does not pull WeddingWire favorites in for you.

**Onboarded to Envoy** means the business confirmed this listing. **Unverified listing** means someone else added it and the business has not confirmed it. It is OK to email an unverified listing — check the address yourself first. Neither badge tells you when they will reply, and neither one is a couple review. Read those on WeddingWire. See [Contact badges explained](/help-center/contacts/contact-badges-explained).

### Chat

On the **Chat** tab, ask what to include when you write to a venue, how to sequence photographer and florist emails, or what "budget" should mean in the first note. The answers stay tied to this wedding. When you finish planning, Envoy prepares outreach drafts for the attached contacts.

### A pass through one wedding

**Overview:** a Charleston weekend wedding, about 120 guests. Venue and photographer first — one from search, one you typed in from a WeddingWire listing — plus budget and the date you need answers. **Chat:** "What should we ask the venue that isn't on the listing?" **Outreach:** draft, **Revise with Envoy**, **Approve & Send**. The reply is in your inbox and on **Outreach**. WeddingWire stays open for the side-by-side comparison.

### Outreach from your inbox

Envoy only uses email related to the contacts you selected. It prepares drafts, sends what you approve, and shows those threads in **Outreach**. Disconnect anytime from **Account**.

Connect Gmail or Outlook in **Connected Email Accounts**. The recipient sees your email address.

**Envoy is for** couples and planners emailing for them who want several wedding vendors, one brief, and the thread in their own email.

## How local vendor discovery works

WeddingWire's strength is **the local listing**. You compare wedding professionals in a category, read reviews, check the photos and the pricing or availability on the profile, and request a quote without leaving the directory.

Envoy's strength is **discovery that becomes a project**. Search is driven by your description and location, and the people you keep are Contacts on the wedding. You can do the first search before you sign up. WeddingWire remains the catalog you browse by category.

Start on WeddingWire when you want the marketplace view of a city. Move the people you actually might email into Envoy.

## How a wedding stays organized

WeddingWire organizes the world as **vendors and inquiries**. Each request hangs off a listing. Planning tools help you track the wedding, but the communication record is the marketplace thread for that professional.

Envoy organizes the world as **one wedding**. Location, budget, and deadline sit beside every contact. **Overview** is the record of who is on the project. **Chat** is where you work out the ask. When the caterer and the venue both need the same date and headcount, you are updating one project, then writing two emails that share it.

## How quote requests compare with email outreach

On WeddingWire, you choose a listing and send a **quote request or message**. It is a fast first look when you want several photographers to respond in the product. The thread stays on WeddingWire.

On Envoy, you choose a contact — from search or one you already know — and send an **email you approve**. **Revise with Envoy** shortens a draft or fills a gap.

Follow-up follows the same split. WeddingWire replies stay in WeddingWire. Envoy replies stay in your inbox and on the **Outreach** tab.

## Directory leads and direct outreach

Searching for wedding vendors is free for couples before and after you sign up. You can keep searching from **Dashboard**, project creation, and **Contacts**. These Help pages aren't a full pricing sheet — plan or account options show in the product when you sign in with Google or Outlook. You add the contact, and you send the email.

<Note>
  **A typical planning week:** guests and the registry on Zola or Joy, high-volume browsing on The Knot, local listing comparisons on WeddingWire, shortlist and email on Envoy, the pro's contract in HoneyBook.
</Note>

## Conclusion

Keep WeddingWire when you want to compare local vendors and request a quote. Use Envoy when you're ready to email that shortlist. You don't have to switch. For browsing a famous directory at volume, use The Knot. This page stays on the local listing.

### Comparison matrix

This isn't a winner-take-all score — each product wins the job it's built for. WeddingWire's rows come first, in plain terms: compare local listings, read local reviews, request a quote. That is not "The Knot, again." Envoy's rows are the project and your email. You choose listings on WeddingWire too.

| Category | Envoy | WeddingWire | Best for… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compare local vendors side by side | A search for your city that you save onto a project. If a name is missing, add it yourself | Local profiles next to each other: photos, pricing or availability, and a way to request a quote | **WeddingWire** |
| Couple reviews on local vendors | No review library. Badges only say whether the business confirmed the listing | What other couples wrote on the local listing you are comparing | **WeddingWire** |
| Email the shortlist yourself | A Gmail or Outlook note you approve, to someone you found or already knew. Browsing is free for couples on both. Vendors often pay for leads on WeddingWire. On Envoy the email is yours | A quote request or message on the listing | **Envoy** |
| Every vendor on one wedding | Shared date, city, budget, and deadline next to each contact | A separate listing and inquiry per vendor | **Envoy** |
| Questions for this wedding only | **Chat** uses the details you saved before a draft exists | Planning ideas beside the directory | **Envoy** |
| Start a shortlist before an account | Up to 8 vendors before you create an account. Add more after you sign in | Public listings you can open while you compare | **Envoy** |
| A vendor you already have | Name and email with **+ New contact**, or **+ Attach existing** | The WeddingWire profile stays there until you add them on the project | **Envoy** |
| Where the reply lives | Your inbox and **Outreach** for that contact | The WeddingWire thread on that listing | **Envoy** |

<Tip>
  Keep WeddingWire open to compare local listings and request quotes. Use Envoy when you want those vendors in one project and in your email. You don't have to switch. The 8-vendor limit is only the shortlist before you create an account.
</Tip>
