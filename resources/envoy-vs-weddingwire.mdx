Use **WeddingWire** and **Envoy** together. Planners already research on a wedding directory and then email vendors themselves. WeddingWire is where you compare local listings. Envoy is where those vendors become a project and the email goes out from your inbox.

**Contacts** (your vendors / service providers) are the businesses on the wedding — venue, photographer, florist, caterer — not a personal address book.

WeddingWire is part of The Knot Worldwide, the same company as [The Knot](/resources/envoy-vs-the-knot). This page is not The Knot with another logo. The Knot page is for brand reach and browsing vendors at volume. WeddingWire is the sibling directory, and it exists here for the local listing: photos, couple reviews, pricing or availability, and a quote request. The catalogs overlap. If the names look familiar, that is the shared company. Open this page when the next step is a local profile, not another tour of the big brand.

The [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) is at the end.

## What WeddingWire is

WeddingWire is a directory built so couples can **find wedding vendors** near them and compare listings before they book. The about-the-brand promise is search, comparison, and a path to local professionals — venues through photographers and everyone in between.

### Wedding vendor directory

You search by the kind of professional and the place you are getting married. Listings are meant to be compared side by side: photos, couple reviews, and pricing or availability details when the business has added them. That listing page is the product. It answers "who works in my area, and what do other couples say?"

### Planning tools, ideas, and community

WeddingWire also offers planning tools, inspiration, and a reviews community around the directory. Couples use them to keep a checklist in view while they are still requesting information. The center of gravity remains the marketplace: listings, reviews, and quote requests.

### The Knot Worldwide and regional discovery

WeddingWire sits inside The Knot Worldwide with The Knot and regional sister marketplaces. For couples, that means wedding-specific discovery that is local first, and a company family that operates wedding marketplaces in more than one country. You may recognize businesses you have also seen on The Knot. The overlap is real. WeddingWire's day-to-day job is still the local listing: compare, read reviews, and request a quote.

### How couples and listed businesses use it

Couples search the directory and request information from the profiles they like. The conversation starts as a WeddingWire inquiry.

You choose which listings to message. Browsing is free for couples. Vendors often pay for leads. On Envoy the email is yours. Choice is not the split — a marketplace request versus your own email is.

**WeddingWire is for** couples and planners who want local wedding listings, couple reviews, and quote requests in that marketplace.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for the person **planning the wedding as a project** and contacting service providers directly. In Envoy those businesses are **Contacts**. A wedding project can hold the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and the rest together.

Keep the guest website, registry, and RSVPs on Zola or Joy. Envoy is where vendor outreach lives.

A planner can send for the couple from the inbox they connect, with the wedding's details on the project.

### Project workspace

Open the project and you get three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | Project details — location, budget, deadline — and the contacts attached to this wedding |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy using the details you already saved |
| **Outreach** | Drafts, revisions, sends, and replies |

Shared details stay on the project. You do not rebuild the brief every time you move from the florist listing to the caterer listing.

### Contacts and discovery

Describe the wedding on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com) and search by ZIP or postal code with no account. You can select up to **8 contacts** before you sign up. That number is only the pre-signup shortlist, not a limit on the wedding. After sign-in, add more during project creation from **Dashboard**, from **Contacts**, or on the project.

Results depend on what you type and where the wedding is. If your market comes back thin, add the vendor you already booked or saved from WeddingWire. On **Contacts**, use **+ New contact** (name and email). On **Overview**, use **+ New contact** or **+ Attach existing**. Envoy does not pull WeddingWire favorites in automatically.

**Onboarded to Envoy** means the business confirmed a listing on Envoy, which is a better reason to trust that the contact details are theirs. **Unverified listing** means a consumer created or manages the card and the business has not confirmed it. Outreach is still allowed; verify the email the way you would any unconfirmed card. Badges are not a promise of a reply time. Details are in [Contact badges explained](/help-center/contacts/contact-badges-explained). Couple reviews of the kind WeddingWire is known for live on WeddingWire, not on these badges.

### Chat

On the **Chat** tab, ask what to include when you write to a venue, how to sequence photographer and florist emails, or what "budget" should mean in the first note. The answers stay tied to this wedding. When you finish planning, Envoy prepares outreach drafts for the attached contacts.

### A pass through one wedding

**Overview:** the Saturday, the city, the budget, and four contacts — two from search, two you typed in from WeddingWire. **Chat:** "What does the band need that the venue already has?" **Outreach:** a draft to the band, **Revise with Envoy**, **Approve & Send** from the planner's or the couple's Gmail. The reply is in that inbox and on **Outreach**.

### Outreach from your inbox

Envoy only uses email related to the contacts you selected. It prepares drafts, sends what you approve, and shows those threads in **Outreach**. Disconnect anytime from **Account**.

Connect Gmail or Outlook in **Connected Email Accounts**. The recipient sees your email address.

**Envoy is for** couples and planners emailing for them who want several wedding vendors, one brief, and the thread in their own email.

## How local vendor discovery works

WeddingWire's strength is **the local listing**. You compare wedding professionals in a category, read reviews, check the photos and the pricing or availability the business published, and request information without leaving the directory. Because WeddingWire is part of The Knot Worldwide, that kind of wedding marketplace also shows up across the company's regional brands. This is discovery at the listing level.

Envoy's strength is **discovery that becomes a project**. Search is driven by your description and location, and the people you keep are Contacts on the wedding. You can do the first search before you sign up. WeddingWire remains the catalog you browse by category.

Start on WeddingWire when you want the marketplace view of a city. Move the people you actually might email into Envoy.

## How a wedding stays organized

WeddingWire organizes the world as **vendors and inquiries**. Each request hangs off a listing. Planning tools help you track the wedding, but the communication record is the marketplace thread for that professional.

Envoy organizes the world as **one wedding**. Location, budget, and deadline sit beside every contact. **Overview** is the record of who is on the project. **Chat** is where you work out the ask. When the caterer and the venue both need the same date and headcount, you are updating one project, then writing two emails that share it.

## How quote requests compare with email outreach

On WeddingWire, you choose a listing and send a **quote request or message**. It is a fast first look when you want several photographers to respond in the product. The thread stays on WeddingWire.

On Envoy, you choose a contact — from search or one you already know — and send an **email you approve**. **Revise with Envoy** shortens a draft or fills a gap. Browsing is free for couples on both. Vendors often pay for leads on WeddingWire. On Envoy the email is yours.

Follow-up follows the same split. WeddingWire replies stay in WeddingWire. Envoy replies stay in your inbox and on the **Outreach** tab.

## Directory leads and direct outreach

WeddingWire's listings are deep because vendors invest in placement and leads. That cost sits with the business. Couples browse for free.

Envoy homepage search is free before an account. Sign in with Google or Outlook and Envoy shows the account details for using the product. You add the contact, and you send the email.

<Note>
  **A typical planning week:** guests and the registry on Zola or Joy, high-volume browsing on The Knot, local listing comparisons on WeddingWire, shortlist and email on Envoy, the pro's contract in HoneyBook.
</Note>

## Conclusion

Read WeddingWire for the local profile: reviews, photos, pricing details, and a quote request, inside The Knot Worldwide. Read [Envoy vs The Knot](/resources/envoy-vs-the-knot) when the question is directory scale. Use Envoy for the project and the email you actually send. The matrix marks one WeddingWire row — local listings — and leaves the inbox work with Envoy.

### Comparison matrix

Couples pick listings on WeddingWire. The email row is about a quote request in the marketplace versus a message from your inbox. WeddingWire's single edge is the local listing itself.

| Category | Envoy | WeddingWire | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| The whole vendor list on one wedding | Shared date, city, budget, and deadline beside every contact you will email | A separate listing and inquiry per professional | **Envoy** |
| Local listings and quote requests | A search that becomes a project shortlist for your city. Coverage follows your description; add anyone missing by name | Side-by-side local profiles — couple reviews, photos, pricing or availability — and a quote request, with regional reach through The Knot Worldwide | **WeddingWire** |
| Who the message comes from | An email you approve, from Gmail or Outlook, to someone you searched or typed in | A quote request or message on the listing. Vendors often pay for that lead | **Envoy** |
| What to ask this wedding | **Chat** on this project's details before the draft exists | Planning ideas and checklists next to the directory | **Envoy** |
| Before you create an account | Free homepage search. Up to 8 contacts only until you sign in; the project can hold more after that | Public listings you can open while you compare a category | **Envoy** |
| A vendor you already have | Enter name and email with **+ New contact**, or attach someone already in your directory | The profile stays on WeddingWire until you add them on the Envoy project | **Envoy** |
| The reply thread | Your inbox, synced to **Outreach** for that contact | The WeddingWire thread on that listing | **Envoy** |

<Tip>
  Compare the local profile on WeddingWire, then put the vendors you will actually email on an Envoy project. Homepage search is free; the 8-contact cap ends at sign-in.
</Tip>

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can my planner run this for us?">
    Yes. They can build the project, attach the vendors, and send from their connected Gmail or Outlook while the wedding details stay in one place.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can we keep WeddingWire saves and still use Envoy?">
    Yes. Leave the reviews and quote requests on WeddingWire. Add the businesses you want on the wedding as Contacts. If you already have their email, use **+ New contact** instead of waiting on search.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens to replies?">
    Replies arrive in the inbox that sent the note and appear on **Outreach**. Envoy's access is limited to email related to the contacts you selected. You can disconnect from **Account**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
