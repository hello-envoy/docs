**Envoy vs WeddingWire** depends on whether you are still discovering local wedding vendors or already coordinating the people you might hire. WeddingWire is a wedding marketplace for searching, comparing, and requesting information from local professionals. Envoy is the project workspace where those people become **Contacts** and vendor outreach goes out from your own email.

WeddingWire is part of The Knot Worldwide, the same company as [The Knot](/resources/envoy-vs-the-knot), so the directories overlap. This page treats them as siblings with different strengths, then closes with a [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix).

## What WeddingWire is

WeddingWire is a directory built so couples can **find wedding vendors** near them and compare listings before they book. The about-the-brand promise is search, comparison, and a path to local professionals — venues through photographers and everyone in between.

### Wedding vendor directory

You search by the kind of professional and the place you are getting married. Listings are meant to be compared side by side: photos, couple reviews, and pricing or availability details when the business has added them. That listing page is the product. It answers "who works in my area, and what do other couples say?"

### Planning tools, ideas, and community

WeddingWire also offers planning tools, inspiration, and a reviews community around the directory. Couples use them to keep a checklist in view while they are still requesting information. The center of gravity remains the marketplace: listings, reviews, and quote requests.

### The Knot Worldwide and regional discovery

WeddingWire sits inside The Knot Worldwide with The Knot and regional sister marketplaces. For couples, that means wedding-specific discovery that is local first, and a company family that operates wedding marketplaces in more than one country. You may recognize businesses you have also seen on The Knot. The overlap is real. WeddingWire's day-to-day job is still the local listing: compare, read reviews, and request a quote.

### How couples and listed businesses use it

Couples search the directory and request information from the profiles they like. The conversation starts as a WeddingWire inquiry.

Vendors use premium listings and lead products to reach those couples. Browsing is for you. Distribution, for many businesses, is paid advertising and leads. That is a standard wedding-marketplace model, and it is a good fit when you want pros to respond inside WeddingWire.

**WeddingWire is for** couples who want a wedding-specific directory of local professionals, with reviews and listing details, and who are happy to request quotes in that marketplace.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for the person **planning the wedding as a project** and contacting service providers directly. In Envoy those businesses are **Contacts**. A wedding project can hold the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and the rest together.

The guest website, registry, and RSVP list are not part of Envoy. If you want those, keep them on a product made for guests. Envoy is where the vendor outreach lives.

### Project workspace

Open the project and you get three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | Project details — location, budget, deadline — and the contacts attached to this wedding |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy using the details you already saved |
| **Outreach** | Drafts, revisions, sends, and replies |

Shared details stay on the project. You do not rebuild the brief every time you move from the florist listing to the caterer listing.

### Contacts and discovery

Describe the wedding on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com) and search by ZIP or postal code with no account. Review badges such as **Onboarded to Envoy** and **Unverified listing**, and select up to 8 contacts to carry into signup. Signed-in search is available from **Dashboard** during project creation, and from the **Contacts** page later.

You are assembling a shortlist. WeddingWire is assembling a directory you can query.

### Chat

On the **Chat** tab, ask what to include when you write to a venue, how to sequence photographer and florist emails, or what "budget" should mean in the first note. The answers stay tied to this wedding. When you finish planning, Envoy prepares outreach drafts for the attached contacts.

### Outreach from your inbox

Connect Gmail or Outlook in **Connected Email Accounts**. Edit the draft, use **Revise with Envoy**, and **Approve & Send**. The recipient sees your email address. Their reply shows up in your inbox and on **Outreach**.

**Envoy is for** couples and anyone coordinating on their behalf who want several wedding contacts, one brief, and a paper trail in their own email.

## How local vendor discovery works

WeddingWire's strength is **the local listing**. You compare wedding professionals in a category, read reviews, check the photos and the pricing or availability the business published, and request information without leaving the directory. Because WeddingWire is part of The Knot Worldwide, that kind of wedding marketplace also shows up across the company's regional brands. This is discovery at the listing level.

Envoy's strength is **discovery that becomes a project**. Search is driven by your description and location, and the people you keep are Contacts on the wedding. You can do the first search before you sign up. WeddingWire remains the catalog you browse by category.

Start on WeddingWire when you want the marketplace view of a city. Move the people you actually might email into Envoy.

## How a wedding stays organized

WeddingWire organizes the world as **vendors and inquiries**. Each request hangs off a listing. Planning tools help you track the wedding, but the communication record is the marketplace thread for that professional.

Envoy organizes the world as **one wedding**. Location, budget, and deadline sit beside every contact. **Overview** is the record of who is on the project. **Chat** is where you work out the ask. When the caterer and the venue both need the same date and headcount, you are updating one project, then writing two emails that share it.

## How quote requests compare with email outreach

On WeddingWire, the usual first move is a **quote request or message** to a listing. It is fast when you want several photographers to respond in the product. Those businesses may be paying for the lead. You are not writing from your personal inbox, and the thread stays on WeddingWire.

On Envoy, the first move is an **email you approve**. Envoy drafts it from the project; **Revise with Envoy** is there if the draft is too long or missing a detail. You send it from Gmail or Outlook. Couples do not pay a lead fee either way. The difference is who owns the introduction: a marketplace lead, or your message to a contact you chose.

Follow-up follows the same split. WeddingWire replies stay in WeddingWire. Envoy replies stay in your inbox and on the **Outreach** tab.

## Directory leads and direct outreach

WeddingWire's model rewards vendors who invest in listings and leads, which is why the directory is deep enough to compare. That cost is on the business, not on the couple browsing.

Search on the Envoy homepage is free before you create an account. You sign in with Google or Outlook to save the project and send mail. You choose the contact, and you send the email.

## Conclusion

**WeddingWire** is the stronger fit when you want a wedding marketplace: local listings, reviews, pricing details, and quote requests, including the regional reach of The Knot Worldwide. **Envoy** is the stronger fit when wedding planning turns into coordination — several contacts, one project, vendor outreach from your own inbox.

The Knot is the better page if your question is pure directory scale and brand reach. WeddingWire is the better page if your question is the local listing and the quote. Envoy is the better page if your question is the project and the email. Using WeddingWire to research and Envoy to write is a clean split.

### Comparison matrix

Envoy has the edge on the project and on outreach. WeddingWire has the edge on one category: discovering and comparing local wedding vendors in a marketplace built for that.

| Category | Envoy | WeddingWire | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-contact project workspace | Venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and others on one project with shared details | Each professional is a listing and an inquiry | **Envoy** |
| Regional vendor discovery | Contact search for your wedding and location, saved onto a project | A wedding marketplace for comparing local listings — reviews, photos, pricing or availability — with regional reach through The Knot Worldwide | **WeddingWire** |
| You choose who to contact | You decide who is a contact and who gets an email | You choose listings to message; vendors invest in placement and leads so couples can find them | **Envoy** |
| Outreach from your own email | You approve a draft and send it from Gmail or Outlook | Quote requests and messages on WeddingWire | **Envoy** |
| Project-scoped planning | **Chat** uses this project's location, budget, deadline, and contacts | Planning tools and ideas beside the directory | **Envoy** |
| Contact shortlist before signup | Build a shortlist on the homepage before an account; search again from **Dashboard** | Browse public listings while you compare options in a category | **Envoy** |
| Follow-up in your inbox | Replies in your email and in **Outreach** | Replies in the WeddingWire thread for that listing | **Envoy** |

<Tip>
  Search for wedding contacts on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com) before you sign up, then keep the ones you choose on a single project. Use WeddingWire when you want the local directory view.
</Tip>
