Aisle Planner is software for wedding and event professionals. Envoy is where you find service providers for one wedding and email them from your own inbox. If your planner invited you to Aisle Planner, keep using it. You don't have to switch.

Service providers (called **Contacts** in Envoy) are the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and the other businesses on your wedding. They are not your guest list, and they are not the client list inside a planner's studio software.

<Note>
  **Planners can keep both.** Aisle Planner runs the studio: leads, proposals, contracts, invoices, checklists, timelines, and the client portal. Envoy is for one wedding when you are finding service providers and emailing them from your inbox.
</Note>

## Aisle Planner vs Envoy

Aisle Planner is an all-in-one business and planning tool that a wedding professional pays for and shares with clients. Envoy is a wedding project where you find service providers, plan what to ask, and send email you approve from Gmail or Microsoft. A planner can use Aisle Planner for the studio and Envoy for outreach on a client's wedding.

## At a glance

- **Keep Aisle Planner for** proposals, contracts, invoices, online payments, checklists, timelines, budgets, design boards, floor plans, seating, and the client portal.
- **Use Envoy for** finding service providers near the wedding and emailing them from your own inbox, with the wedding's details behind every note.
- **Don't expect Envoy to** send proposals or invoices, collect payments, build a seating chart, or run a client portal. Those stay in Aisle Planner or the other tools you use.

The [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) is at the end. Aisle Planner wins the business and day-of planning rows. Envoy wins the finding and outreach rows.

## What Aisle Planner is

Aisle Planner is software for event professionals, especially wedding planners. A planning business uses it to bring in leads, book clients, and plan each event in one login. Florists, venues, photographers, and other wedding pros use it too.

### Leads, proposals, and payments

The sales tools cover the path from first inquiry to booked client. A business can track leads, send questionnaires and brochures, and send proposals and interactive quotes. Contracts with e-signatures, invoices, and online payments sit in the same place.

### Planning tools for each event

Each client's wedding is a project with its own dashboard and calendar. Planners build checklists, timelines, and budgets, and keep service provider details and files with the project. Guest management, an event website with online RSVP, and layouts and seating are part of the same planning tools.

### Design boards and floor plans

The **Design Studio** holds style guides, mood boards, and color palettes. Planners can also sketch layouts and floor plans for the ceremony and reception spaces.

### Client portal and team sharing

The planner invites the couple to a client portal. The couple sees the tools and details the planner chooses to share, and can comment and check progress. Team members and other wedding pros can be invited too, with permission settings for each person.

### On a phone

Aisle Planner runs in a web browser, including on phones and tablets. Some tools, such as layouts and drag-and-drop in the checklist, budget, and seating tools, work best on a computer.

**Aisle Planner is for** wedding planners and other event pros who want their business and their clients' planning in one paid tool.

## What Envoy is

Envoy is for **running the wedding as a project** and reaching out to the service providers on it. You might be the couple. You might be a friend, a family member, or a planner acting for that couple. Either way, the email comes from the inbox you connect.

Aisle Planner and Envoy stay separate logins. Proposals, contracts, invoices, and the client portal stay in Aisle Planner. Envoy is the finding and outreach step for one wedding.

### Project workspace

Each wedding opens on **Today**, with **Contacts** and **Project** beside it:

| Place | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Today** | Opens first and shows the wedding name and date. |
| **Contacts** | **Inbox** for email. **All** for the contacts on this wedding. |
| **Project** | The wedding name and date, and **Edit project details**. |
| **Ask Envoy** | Plan before you contact anyone. On a larger screen it sits on the left side. On a phone, tap the Envoy logo in the top bar. |

Nothing on those tabs is a checklist, a seating chart, a proposal, or an invoice. The location, budget, and wedding date you save stay attached to every contact on the wedding. Envoy calls that date **Deadline**.

### Contacts and discovery

On the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), describe the wedding and enter a ZIP or postal code before you create an account. Results are grouped by category, such as florist, caterer, photographer, etc. You can start a shortlist of up to 8 before you create an account. After you sign in, add more from **Dashboard**, **Contacts**, or the project.

**Onboarded to Envoy** means the business confirmed this listing. **Unverified listing** means someone else added it and the business has not confirmed it. It is OK to email an unverified listing after you check the address yourself. See [Contact badges explained](/help-center/contacts/contact-badges-explained).

You can also add people you already know. Click **+ New contact** and enter a name and email. To bring in a longer list, use **Import from file** and upload a `.csv` or `.xlsx` file. A planner's favorite florist, saved for one wedding, can join the next one with **+ Attach existing**. See [Add or upload contacts](/help-center/contacts/add-or-upload-contacts).

### Chat

**Ask Envoy** is scoped to this wedding. It uses the title, description, location, budget, and wedding date (**Deadline**) you saved. Ask what a caterer needs to quote, or what to send the venue first. When planning is complete, Envoy prepares a draft for each contact on the wedding.

### A pass through one wedding

**Project details:** a Charleston weekend wedding, about 120 guests. Venue and photographer first, then florist and caterer. The planner keeps the client's timeline and invoices in Aisle Planner. **Ask Envoy:** "What should the first note to the florist include?" **Inbox:** Click **New message**, then **Create draft**. Envoy drafts a first pass. Use the **Draft with Envoy** bar if the note needs a change, then click **Approve & Send**. The reply lands in your mailbox and on **Inbox**.

### Outreach from your inbox

Connect Gmail or Microsoft under **Connected Email Accounts**. The message comes from your address and is signed with your name. You approve every send. Envoy only uses email related to the contacts you selected. Disconnect the inbox anytime from **Settings**.

**Envoy is for** the couple, or a planner emailing for them, who wants to find the right people and reach them from their own inbox.

## Who each product is for

| | Envoy | Aisle Planner |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical person** | Couple, or someone coordinating for the couple | Wedding planner or other event pro |
| **What it's for** | One wedding project and its contacts | The business's leads, clients, and events |
| **Successful outcome** | The right service providers are emailed, and replies are in your inbox | A client is booked, paid, and planned from proposal to wedding day |

Both can appear on the same wedding. They do not replace each other.

## How you find service providers

Envoy starts with **finding**. Describe the wedding and the location, and Envoy shows service providers by category. You pick who goes on the shortlist, and you can add anyone you already know.

Aisle Planner is built to organize the service providers already on a project and invite them to collaborate. Most planners add people they already know. Envoy starts with a search near the wedding.

## How you plan the wedding

Aisle Planner is the deeper planning tool. Checklists, timelines, budgets, design boards, floor plans, seating, and an RSVP website all sit on the client's project. If you need a run-of-show or a seating chart, Aisle Planner does that job and Envoy does not.

Envoy's planning is about **what to ask**. **Ask Envoy** helps you decide the order of asks, what each contact needs to hear, and when you need answers. It uses the wedding date you saved (**Deadline**). That is not a wedding-day schedule.

## How outreach and replies work

On Envoy, each contact gets a personal note drafted from the wedding's details. Click **Create draft** for the first pass. Review it, use the **Draft with Envoy** bar if you want a rewrite, and click **Approve & Send**. It sends from your Gmail or Microsoft address, and replies come back to your inbox and to **Inbox**.

Aisle Planner also has email integration, templates, and automatic replies for the business's leads and clients. Couples and team members get notifications and comments inside the portal. Envoy's difference is a personal inquiry you approve, sent from your own inbox, with the thread on that wedding.

## What each side pays for

Searching for service providers on Envoy is free for couples before and after you sign up. You can also start a shortlist of up to 8 before you create an account.

Aisle Planner is software the **business** pays for. Plans are monthly and priced by the number of active projects, and each comes with a free 30-day trial that needs a credit card. Couples usually get in through their planner's client portal. Check [Aisle Planner's pricing page](https://www.aisleplanner.com/pricing) for current plans.

<Note>
  **A typical planning week:** the client's checklist, timeline, and invoices in Aisle Planner, the guest list and registry on Zola or Joy, and outreach to new service providers on Envoy.
</Note>

## Coming soon to Envoy

These Envoy features are on the way. They aren't in the app yet, and the page will change when they arrive.

- **Timeline and tasks:** on **Project**, a planning **Timeline** shows milestones by month (for example, book the venue or lock catering) and opens each one into its tasks. You can add a task and give it an owner and a due date. This is a planning calendar of tasks, not a wedding-day run-of-show or seating chart.
- **Due this week on Today:** **Today** lists the tasks **Due this week** and the emails that **Need attention**, so you can see the wedding's next steps when you open it.
- **Budget:** on **Project**, a **Budget** view shows what's budgeted, booked, spent, and remaining, with a chart of where the money goes. A **Deposits and payments** list keeps each deposit in date order, and you can mark a deposit paid after you pay it elsewhere. Envoy doesn't take payments.
- **Compare:** under **Contacts**, pick a type of service provider, such as florists, and check two to four of them to see them side by side. Comparing never sends an email.
- **More on Dashboard:** for planners with several weddings, **Dashboard** shows a **Needs you** list across all of them, days to go for each wedding, and a **Next 12 months** view.

## Conclusion

Keep Aisle Planner if you run a planning business and want proposals, invoices, design boards, and day-of planning in one place. Use Envoy when you want to find service providers for a wedding and email them from your own inbox. A planner can be in both: Aisle Planner for the studio, Envoy for one wedding's outreach.
