**Envoy vs HoneyBook** is mostly an audience question. HoneyBook is a business system for wedding professionals — planners, photographers, florists, and similar pros — with contracts, invoices, and a client portal. Envoy is a project workspace for the **person running the wedding**: usually the couple, or someone coordinating for them, who needs to find **Contacts** and send vendor outreach from their own email.

If you are hiring people, you are in Envoy's audience. If you are the business they hire, HoneyBook is built for you. The [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) below is written for someone planning a wedding and contacting service providers, and it gives HoneyBook the one category that matches a pro's business.

## What HoneyBook is

HoneyBook is client-management software for a service business. Wedding planners and creative pros use it to take a lead from first inquiry through a booked client. The couple may receive a proposal link or a portal login. They are the client in the system, not the operator of it.

### Pipeline and inquiries

The pipeline is how a studio tracks people who might book. Inquiries, follow-ups, and stages live on the business side. That is the opposite of a couple searching for vendors: here the professional is organizing their own demand.

### Proposals and contracts

HoneyBook is where the pro sends a proposal and a contract. Scope, price, and terms go out as business documents. The couple reviews and signs what the studio sent. Those documents belong to the pro's business system.

### Invoices, payments, and files

Invoices and payments sit on the same client record, so a deposit and a balance are part of the booking. A client portal gives the couple a place to see files and next steps the pro has shared. This is the back office of a photography studio or a planning business.

### Workflows for the studio

Pros use HoneyBook to standardize how their business replies, sends a contract, and asks for payment. Those workflows serve the studio's leads and bookings.

**HoneyBook is for** wedding professionals running their own client work: proposals, contracts, invoices, and a portal under their business.

## What Envoy is

Envoy's consumer product is for **assembling the wedding and reaching out**. Service providers on the project are **Contacts**. You might be the couple. You might be a friend, a family member, or a planner acting for that couple. Either way, the workspace is the wedding project, and the email comes from the inbox you connect.

A planner who also runs a studio may use HoneyBook for their clients and Envoy when they are the ones discovering and emailing contacts for a specific wedding. Those are different logins for different jobs.

If you are a service provider who wants couples to find you inside Envoy, that is a **Pro** account. See [Join as a Pro](/help-center/pros/join-as-a-pro). That registration is how you receive project inquiries. Contracts, invoices, and client portals stay in the pro's own business tools.

### Project workspace

The wedding is a project with three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | Location, budget, deadline, and the contacts for this wedding |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy before you contact anyone |
| **Outreach** | Drafts you review and send, then the replies |

Nothing on those tabs is a proposal, a contract, or an invoice. The professional you hire can still send those from HoneyBook.

### Contacts and discovery

Search on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com) before you create an account. Describe the wedding, enter a location, and select up to 8 contacts. Badges such as **Onboarded to Envoy** show how a listing is presented. After signup, search from **Dashboard** during project creation or add people from **Contacts**.

You choose the venue, florist, photographer, and caterer. You are not waiting for a studio's pipeline to decide the next step.

### Chat

**Chat** is scoped to your project details. Use it to figure out what to ask a planner versus a photographer, and which details belong in the first email. When planning is complete, Envoy prepares drafts for the contacts on the project.

### Outreach from your inbox

Connect Gmail or Outlook in **Connected Email Accounts**. **Revise with Envoy** if the draft needs a different tone. **Approve & Send** sends from your address. The photographer replies to you. The thread stays in your inbox and on **Outreach**. If they later send a contract, that file comes from their business tools.

**Envoy is for** people coordinating a wedding who want to choose contacts and email them with the project behind the message.

## Who each product is for

| | Envoy | HoneyBook |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical person** | Couple, or someone coordinating for the couple | Planner, photographer, designer, or other wedding pro |
| **Unit of work** | One wedding project and its contacts | The pro's leads and booked clients |
| **Successful outcome** | The right people have been emailed, and replies are in your inbox | A client has a proposal, a contract, and an invoice |

Both can appear on the same wedding. They do not replace each other. The couple's search and the studio's CRM are different products.

## Contracts, invoices, and client portals

HoneyBook's edge is this section. A working studio needs a way to send scope and price, get a signature, collect a deposit, and give the couple a portal. HoneyBook is built as that system. Couples feel it as "our photographer sent a HoneyBook link."

Envoy stops at coordination and email. You can attach files on outreach, and you can keep the thread, but the product will not turn that thread into a contract, an invoice, or a client portal. Expect those from the pro, in whatever system they run — often HoneyBook.

## How a couple or coordinator reaches out

Envoy is the fit when **you** are the one finding wedding vendors. Homepage search works before you sign up. The shortlist becomes Contacts on one project so the venue and the florist share a location, budget, and deadline. **Chat** prepares the questions. Outreach sends the email only after you approve it.

HoneyBook organizes the clients who come to a business that already exists. Opened from the couple's side, it is client-management software shaped for the studio. Envoy is the wedding project with contact discovery.

Someone coordinating **for** a couple can still be the Envoy sender. They connect their own inbox, choose the contacts, and write with the couple's project details. Their HoneyBook account, if they have one, remains the place they invoice **their** clients.

## What each side pays attention to

Couples do not buy HoneyBook to plan a guest list, and they do not pay Envoy lead fees to email a florist. Envoy contact search is free on the homepage before you create an account. You sign in with Google or Outlook to save the project and send mail.

HoneyBook is a subscription the **business** pays so the studio can run proposals, contracts, and invoices. That cost matches a pro CRM. It is the wrong line item for a couple who needs vendor outreach.

## Conclusion

**HoneyBook** is the stronger product when you run a wedding business and need a CRM: contracts, invoices, and a client portal. **Envoy** is the stronger product when you are planning a wedding and need a multi-contact workspace, contacts you choose, and vendor outreach from your own inbox.

Use both on opposite sides of the same wedding. The couple or coordinator uses Envoy to find people and email them. The photographer or planner uses HoneyBook to run the business relationship that follows.

### Comparison matrix

Read this matrix as a couple or coordinator contacting service providers. Envoy is the stronger fit in those rows. HoneyBook is the stronger fit in exactly one row: the pro CRM a wedding business needs. If that row is the only one that describes your job, choose HoneyBook.

| Category | Envoy | HoneyBook | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-contact project workspace | One wedding holds every contact you will email, with shared location, budget, and deadline | A pipeline of the studio's own leads and clients | **Envoy** |
| CRM for a wedding business | Consumer workspace for the wedding project. A **Pro** account receives inquiries. Contracts and invoices stay in a business CRM | Proposals, contracts, invoices, payments, and a client portal for the pro | **HoneyBook** |
| You choose who to contact | You search and decide which contacts to email | The pro manages incoming client inquiries for their business | **Envoy** |
| Outreach from your own email | You approve every draft and send from Gmail or Outlook, with **Revise with Envoy** | Client messages, files, and portal updates from the studio's account | **Envoy** |
| Project-scoped planning | **Chat** uses this wedding's saved details before outreach | Workflows for how the business books and bills a client | **Envoy** |
| Contact shortlist before signup | Homepage search and up to 8 contacts before you create an account | The workspace is the business's HoneyBook account | **Envoy** |
| Follow-up in your inbox | Replies from contacts land in your email and on **Outreach** | Follow-up lives in the pro's client record and portal | **Envoy** |

</Tip>
