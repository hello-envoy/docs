Use **HoneyBook** and **Envoy** for different jobs, often in the same week. HoneyBook is the studio system for wedding professionals: proposals, contracts, invoices, and a client portal. Envoy is the wedding project for the couple, or for a planner emailing vendors on that couple's behalf.

**Contacts** (your vendors / service providers) are the businesses on a specific wedding. In Envoy they are project contacts, not the client list in a CRM and not a personal address book.

<Note>
  **Planners often keep both.** HoneyBook runs the studio: your clients, proposals, contracts, and invoices. Envoy is for one wedding when you are discovering vendors or emailing them from your inbox — including vendors that couple already knows.
</Note>

If you are hiring people, you are in Envoy's audience. If you are the business they hire, HoneyBook is built for you. The [comparison matrix](#comparison-matrix) is written for someone contacting vendors for a wedding. HoneyBook's row is the pro CRM.

## What HoneyBook is

HoneyBook is client-management software for a service business. Wedding planners and creative pros use it to take a lead from first inquiry through a booked client. The couple may receive a proposal link or a portal login. They are the client in the system, not the operator of it.

### Pipeline and inquiries

The pipeline is how a studio tracks people who might book. Inquiries, follow-ups, and stages live on the business side. That is the opposite of a couple searching for vendors: here the professional is organizing their own demand.

### Proposals and contracts

HoneyBook is where the pro sends a proposal and a contract. Scope, price, and terms go out as business documents. The couple reviews and signs what the studio sent. Those documents belong to the pro's business system.

### Invoices, payments, and files

Invoices and payments sit on the same client record, so a deposit and a balance are part of the booking. A client portal gives the couple a place to see files and next steps the pro has shared. This is the back office of a photography studio or a planning business.

### Workflows for the studio

Pros use HoneyBook to standardize how their business replies, sends a contract, and asks for payment. Those workflows serve the studio's leads and bookings.

**HoneyBook is for** wedding professionals running their own client work: proposals, contracts, invoices, and a portal under their business.

## What Envoy is

Envoy's consumer product is for **assembling the wedding and reaching out**. Service providers on the project are **Contacts**. You might be the couple. You might be a friend, a family member, or a planner acting for that couple. Either way, the workspace is the wedding project, and the email comes from the inbox you connect.

HoneyBook and Envoy stay separate logins. The note at the top is the working split: HoneyBook for the planner's own clients, Envoy when that planner is emailing vendors for a wedding.

If you are a service provider who wants couples to find you inside Envoy, that is a **Pro** account. See [Join as a Pro](/help-center/pros/join-as-a-pro). That registration is how you receive project inquiries. Contracts, invoices, and client portals stay in the pro's own business tools.

### Project workspace

The wedding is a project with three tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|-----|---------------------|
| **Overview** | Location, budget, deadline, and the contacts for this wedding |
| **Chat** | Plan with Envoy before you contact anyone |
| **Outreach** | Drafts you review and send, then the replies |

Nothing on those tabs is a proposal, a contract, or an invoice. The professional you hire can still send those from HoneyBook.

### Contacts and discovery

Search on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com) before you create an account. Describe the wedding, enter a location, and select up to **8 contacts**. That limit is the pre-signup shortlist only. After sign-in, add more from **Dashboard**, **Contacts**, or the project. A planner's vendor roster for the wedding is not capped at 8.

**Onboarded to Envoy** means the business confirmed its listing, which is a better signal that the email belongs to them. **Unverified listing** means a consumer created or manages the card and the business has not confirmed it. You can still write; verify the details. Badges do not promise a reply window. See [Contact badges explained](/help-center/contacts/contact-badges-explained).

Add vendors you already know without searching. **+ New contact** takes a name and email. **+ Attach existing** on **Overview** puts someone from your directory onto this wedding. Search coverage follows the description and location; a missing florist gets typed in.

The couple or planner decides who gets the email. That is separate from a studio pipeline deciding which of the planner's own clients to chase in HoneyBook.

### Chat

**Chat** is scoped to your project details. Use it to figure out what to ask a planner versus a photographer, and which details belong in the first email. When planning is complete, Envoy prepares drafts for the contacts on the project.

### A pass through one wedding

**Overview:** the couple's date, city, and budget, plus a photographer you added by email and a florist from search. **Chat:** "What should we ask the florist before we mention the photographer's timeline?" **Outreach:** draft, **Revise with Envoy**, **Approve & Send** from the planner's or the couple's inbox. If the photographer later sends a contract, that document comes from their HoneyBook (or whatever they use), not from Envoy.

### Outreach from your inbox

Envoy only uses email related to the contacts you selected. It drafts, sends messages you approve, and shows those threads in **Outreach**. Disconnect the inbox from **Account** anytime. HoneyBook's client portal is a different login.

Connect Gmail or Outlook in **Connected Email Accounts**.

**Envoy is for** the couple, or a planner emailing for them, with the wedding's details behind each note.

## Who each product is for

| | Envoy | HoneyBook |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical person** | Couple, or someone coordinating for the couple | Planner, photographer, designer, or other wedding pro |
| **Unit of work** | One wedding project and its contacts | The pro's leads and booked clients |
| **Successful outcome** | The right people have been emailed, and replies are in your inbox | A client has a proposal, a contract, and an invoice |

Both can appear on the same wedding. They do not replace each other. The couple's search and the studio's CRM are different products.

## Contracts, invoices, and client portals

A working studio needs a way to send scope and price, get a signature, collect a deposit, and give the couple a portal. HoneyBook is built as that system. Couples see it as "our photographer sent a HoneyBook link."

Envoy stays on coordination and email. You can attach files on outreach and keep the thread. Contracts, invoices, and the client portal stay in the pro's system — often HoneyBook. The matrix gives HoneyBook that row and no other.

## How a couple or coordinator reaches out

Envoy is the fit when **you** are the one finding wedding vendors. Homepage search works before you sign up. The shortlist becomes Contacts on one project so the venue and the florist share a location, budget, and deadline. **Chat** prepares the questions. Outreach sends the email only after you approve it.

HoneyBook organizes the clients who come to a business that already exists. Opened from the couple's side, it is client-management software shaped for the studio. Envoy is the wedding project with contact discovery.

Someone coordinating **for** a couple can still be the Envoy sender. They connect their own inbox, choose the contacts, and write with the couple's project details. Their HoneyBook account, if they have one, remains the place they invoice **their** clients.

## What each side pays for

Browsing is free for couples on marketplace sites and on Envoy. Vendors often pay for leads on those marketplaces. On Envoy the email is yours. Homepage search is free before you sign up. When you sign in with Google or Outlook, account details are shown in the product.

HoneyBook is software the **business** pays for so the studio can run proposals, contracts, and invoices. That belongs on the studio's books. A couple looking for vendor outreach is in Envoy; a planner can be in both.

<Note>
  **A typical planning week:** guests on Joy or Zola, directory browsing on The Knot or WeddingWire, vendor email for this wedding on Envoy, the studio's contracts and invoices in HoneyBook.
</Note>

## Conclusion

HoneyBook is the studio CRM: contracts, invoices, and a client portal. Envoy is the wedding's vendor project and the email from your inbox. A planner can be logged into both. If your only job is running the business, HoneyBook is the row that matches. The matrix marks that once.

### Comparison matrix

Read this as someone emailing vendors for a wedding, including a planner doing that for a couple. HoneyBook takes the CRM row. The other rows are the couple-side project.

| Category | Envoy | HoneyBook | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| This couple's vendor project | One wedding holds every vendor you will email, with shared location, budget, and deadline | The studio's pipeline of its own leads and booked clients | **Envoy** |
| Studio CRM | The consumer project. A **Pro** account on Envoy receives inquiries. Contracts and invoices stay in the business tool | Proposals, contracts, invoices, payments, and a client portal | **HoneyBook** |
| Emailing vendors for one wedding | Search, or add a known vendor by name and email, then approve the send | The pro tracks clients who hired the studio | **Envoy** |
| Whose inbox | Gmail or Outlook you connect. **Revise with Envoy** before you approve. Inbox use is limited to those contacts | The studio's client messages, files, and portal | **Envoy** |
| Prep before the first vendor note | **Chat** on this wedding's saved details | Workflows for how the business books and bills | **Envoy** |
| Before an Envoy account | Free homepage search. Up to 8 contacts only on that pre-signup shortlist; add the rest after sign-in | The workspace is already the business's HoneyBook account | **Envoy** |
| Vendor replies vs client portal | Replies in your inbox and on **Outreach** | Follow-up on the pro's client record and portal | **Envoy** |

<Tip>
  Planning the wedding, or coordinating it for a couple? Start with contact search on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), or add vendors you already know. Running the studio? Keep HoneyBook for contracts and invoices. Service providers who want inquiries on Envoy can [join as a Pro](/help-center/pros/join-as-a-pro).
</Tip>

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can a planner use HoneyBook and Envoy?">
    Yes. HoneyBook for the studio's clients, contracts, and invoices. Envoy when that planner is discovering or emailing vendors for a specific wedding, from the inbox they connect.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can the couple keep The Knot or Zola?">
    Yes. Directory reviews can stay on The Knot or WeddingWire. The registry and guest site can stay on Zola or Joy. Add the vendors you will email into Envoy yourself. There is no import from those products.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens to replies?">
    Vendor replies come back to the Gmail or Outlook account that sent them, and the thread shows on **Outreach**. A HoneyBook portal message stays in HoneyBook. Envoy only uses email related to the contacts you selected.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
