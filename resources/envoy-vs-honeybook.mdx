HoneyBook and Envoy are easy to place side by side and hard to swap. **HoneyBook is for wedding professionals running a business** — planners, photographers, florists, and similar pros managing clients. **Envoy is for the person running the wedding project** — usually the couple, or someone coordinating on the couple's behalf — who needs to find **Contacts** and email them from their own inbox.

If you are hiring people for a wedding, you are in Envoy's audience. If you are the business those people hire, HoneyBook is built for you.

## At a glance

| | **Envoy** | **HoneyBook** |
|---|-----------|---------------|
| **Who it's for** | People planning and coordinating a project | Wedding professionals managing their business |
| **Primary model** | Project workspace + direct outreach | Client management for a service business |
| **Typical user** | A couple, or someone acting for the couple | A planner, photographer, designer, or similar pro |
| **Best for** | Discovering contacts and emailing them about one wedding | Proposals, contracts, invoices, and a client portal |
| **Project context** | Your wedding: location, budget, deadline, and contacts | The pro's pipeline of leads and clients |
| **Communication** | Email from your Gmail or Outlook | The pro's client messages, files, and portal |
| **Planning help** | Project **Chat** and **Revise with Envoy** | Business workflows around bookings and payments |
| **Search before signup** | Yes — homepage contact search | A HoneyBook account is the business workspace |

## What HoneyBook does well

HoneyBook is a capable **business system** for independent wedding pros:

- A pipeline for inquiries and clients
- Proposals and contracts the pro sends to the couple
- Invoices and payments tied to that client work
- A client portal where files and next steps live
- Automations that help a small studio follow up on its own leads

That is the back office of a photography studio or a planning business. It is the right shape of product when **you are the one being hired**.

## What Envoy does differently

Envoy's consumer product points the other direction. You are assembling the wedding and reaching out.

### You are choosing contacts

On the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com), describe the wedding and search by location before you create an account. You pick the venue, florist, photographer, caterer, and anyone else you want to talk to. After you sign in, you can search again from **Dashboard** and keep everyone on the project as **Contacts**.

### One project for the couple's plan

**Overview**, **Chat**, and **Outreach** belong to your wedding. Location, budget, and deadline sit with the contacts who need them. **Chat** helps you decide what to ask. Envoy drafts the emails; **Revise with Envoy** lets you adjust them; you approve the send.

### Your inbox, your thread

Outreach goes out from the Gmail or Outlook account you connect. The photographer replies to you. Envoy does not turn that thread into a contract, an invoice, or a client portal — those stay with the professional's own tools, which may be HoneyBook.

### A planner can still be the sender

Someone coordinating **for** a couple — a friend, a family member, or a planner acting on that wedding — can use Envoy to discover contacts and send outreach from their own inbox. That is still the project workspace. It is separate from the planner's HoneyBook account, where that planner manages **their** clients, proposals, and payments.

## When to use HoneyBook

Choose **HoneyBook** when:

- You are a wedding professional running your own client work
- You need proposals, contracts, invoices, or a client portal
- The job is your pipeline — leads in, bookings out — for the business
- You want the couple to interact with **your** studio process

If you are a service provider who wants to receive project inquiries through Envoy, that is a different account type. See [Join as a Pro](/help-center/pros/join-as-a-pro).

## When to use Envoy

Choose **Envoy** when:

- You are planning a wedding and need to **contact several service providers**
- You want to choose who is on the project and what you say to them
- The wedding's details should live in **one workspace**
- You want email from **your** Gmail or Outlook, with every send approved by you
- You want to search for contacts **before you create an account**

## Can you use both?

Yes, on opposite sides of the same wedding. The couple (or whoever is coordinating for them) uses Envoy to find contacts and send outreach. The photographer or planner uses HoneyBook to run **their** business with that couple — contract, invoice, portal. Envoy does not include contracts, invoicing, or a client portal, and HoneyBook is not a couple's contact-search and inbox workspace.

<Tip>
  Planning the wedding yourself? Start with contact search on the [Envoy homepage](https://app.hello-envoy.com). Running a wedding business? HoneyBook is aimed at that work; Envoy's [Pro registration](/help-center/pros/join-as-a-pro) is the path for service providers on Envoy.
</Tip>
